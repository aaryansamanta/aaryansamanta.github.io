Papers

Published2025

Quantum-Inspired Hybrid Genetic Algorithm and Graph Neural Network Ensemble for Multimodal Classification

IEEE 7th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Manufacturing (AIAM), first author

Combines a quantum-inspired genetic algorithm (qubit-encoded chromosomes updated by quantum-rotation gates) with a graph neural network, joins them with a learned late-fusion layer, and explains predictions with SHAP. In the paper’s experiments it reached F1 0.57, against 0.55 for Random Forest and 0.52 for SVM.

Published2026

Natural Genetic Variation in Mitochondrial Health-Regulating Genes in the C. elegans Strains CX11314 and EG4725 Leads to Increased Mitochondrial Resilience

International Journal of High School Research, first author with Mansi Mandal and Mohammed Yasar Meeran. Capstone of the UC Santa Barbara Summer Research Academy, where I was the youngest accepted scholar.

Why do two wild worm strains resist mitochondrial dysfunction better than the lab strain N2? I combined variant data for 540 isotypes (CaeNDR), consequence prediction with Ensembl VEP, dN/dS selection tests, and 609 RNA-seq samples across six mitochondrial quality-control genes. A neural-network expression predictor reached a Spearman correlation of 0.959.

OngoingJan 2026 to present

Functional imaging of the pelvic floor

Stanford University School of Medicine, Department of Urology. Mentor: Christos E. Constantinou, Associate Professor of Urology, Emeritus.

I analyze anonymized ultrasound from 23 subjects and turn it into displacement, velocity and acceleration curves during voluntary contraction (“the Knack”), Valsalva and cough, a question that matters for stress urinary incontinence. Preliminary result: voluntary contraction increases urethral support, and cough produced no significant caudal displacement. Not yet published.

SubmittedSummer 2026

InfernoCommand: fire-relative reinforcement learning for wildfire response

UCLA COSMOS, Cluster 1: Brain-Inspired Computing

An actor-critic agent (a CNN over a 595 × 316 terrain grid plus an MLP over weather and fleet state) that dispatches firefighting resources. Because actions are defined relative to the fire, it generalizes to ignition points it never trained on. The environment uses SRTM elevation, OpenStreetMap buildings and roads, NOAA weather, and the January 2025 Palisades Fire. Over training, mean reward rose 52% and buildings lost fell 54%. The repository lists its limitations, including synthetic traffic and a few unsourced constants. Manuscript submitted for publication.

In progress2026

When help becomes a hindrance: how simplified biology diagrams create misconceptions

Education research, targeting CBE—Life Sciences Education

Static, single-arrow diagrams may teach students that biology is simpler and more uniform than it is. This three-phase study deconstructs common diagrams, tests redesigned versions against them, and distills design guidelines. Instruments, consent documents and the analysis pipeline are built; data collection has not started.
